Three approaches to hair-stroke brows

Hair-stroke brows use individual lines to suggest natural hair direction. The same broad name can describe different tools and combinations of strokes.

Manual method

A manual blade is used to place strokes around the existing framework. Colour may be darker initially, so discuss healing and retention separately.

Single-instrument method

One instrument is chosen for the existing hair texture. Main and supporting strokes refine the outline and direction. This can be discussed when the aim is subtle definition that sits close to the natural brows.

Dual-instrument method

Two instruments place fine and structural strokes in combination. Where a combination of main and fine strokes is appropriate, main strokes establish structure and hair direction, bringing out the visual relationship between brows and brow bone. Fine strokes then vary density, tone and definition to connect the lines naturally. The aim is a framework with layers and natural hair flow that complements the features, not filling every gap or simply adding darkness.
