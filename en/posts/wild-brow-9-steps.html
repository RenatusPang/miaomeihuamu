Hair-stroke brow design: establish the framework before arranging the strokes

A natural-looking hair-stroke brow is not created by continually adding more lines.

What matters is establishing the overall framework first, then arranging main strokes, supporting strokes and fine strokes according to hair direction, so that structure, density and tone connect naturally within the brow.

In an individual brow design, Lele first observes the person’s facial features, bone structure, brow bone and existing hair flow, then determines a suitable contour and arrangement of strokes.

The following nine paper illustrations show how the structure of a hair-stroke brow is built up step by step.

01｜Identify the direction of hair growth

Before arranging the strokes, identify the direction of hair growth across the whole brow.

Observe how the existing brow hairs grow. A broad curved line can serve as a guide to their overall direction. In this illustration, the line runs through the upper-middle part of the brow body and gradually extends towards the tail.

It is not the outer contour of the brow. It is an important reference for arranging the main strokes that follow.

Establishing the overall direction first helps prevent later strokes from going their separate ways and making the shape look disjointed.

02｜Establish the brow ridge guide

Once the direction of hair growth is clear, identify where the main structural line should sit.

The “brow ridge” in this paper drawing sequence is a structural guide line. It does not refer to the anatomical brow bone.

This line helps establish the brow’s main direction and gives strokes approaching from above and below a common structure to connect to.

Establish a clear relationship between the main strokes before adding other lines. This helps the brow form a coherent framework, rather than a collection of separate marks.

03｜Angle the lower framework back

The main strokes of the lower framework should gradually angle back, following the brow’s overall growth direction.

As the strokes extend from the brow body towards the tail, their angles become flatter and gradually converge towards the back.

The aim is not to draw a particularly straight lower edge, but to let the directions of neighbouring strokes change naturally.

The lower edge should not sit too low or appear too rigid, as this can visually weigh down the relationship between the brows and eyes.

04｜Let the upper framework follow the arch

The direction of the upper main strokes needs to relate to the position of the arch and the overall curve of the brow.

Before the arch, the strokes can open out more freely. After the arch, adjust their direction gradually so that they converge naturally towards the tail.

The aim is to give the brow clear structure without abrupt, rigid changes of direction.

The actual position and curvature of the arch must still be chosen in relation to the individual’s bone structure, the relationship between brows and eyes, and aesthetic preferences. One fixed template cannot suit everyone.

05｜Fan out the brow start

Much of the natural appearance at the brow start comes from variation in stroke direction.

Natural hairs at the brow start do not all grow neatly at the same angle. Follow the existing hair flow when arranging the strokes, allowing them to open out like a fan.

Each stroke has its own direction and spacing, with appropriate variation in density between the lines.

Avoid gathering all the strokes into one bundle or making them overly regular, as this can lose the light, natural character of the original brow hairs.

06｜Check the connections

After arranging the main strokes of the upper and lower frameworks, review how the whole brow connects.

Check whether hair flow is continuous between the brow start, body, arch and tail, and whether strokes approaching from above and below merge naturally into the main structure.

Pay particular attention to the middle of the brow body. The upper and lower strokes should not break off abruptly or clash in direction.

Having structure does not mean every stroke must be neatly aligned.

What matters is how the strokes relate to one another, making the whole brow look continuous and natural.

07｜Add supporting strokes to the lower framework

Once the main-stroke framework is established, look for places within the lower framework that need supporting strokes.

Supporting strokes add detail and density between the main strokes. Their purpose is not to fill every gap.

Consider each added stroke’s length, angle and relationship to the existing hair flow, allowing local density to change gradually.

Keeping both sparse and denser areas within the brow helps express more natural layers of hair growth.

08｜Add supporting strokes to the upper framework

Supporting strokes in the upper framework must also follow the existing hair direction.

Observe the lines around the brow body and arch, adding suitable supporting strokes where needed so that the upper framework connects more naturally with the main structure.

Do not insert strokes across the hair flow merely to fill gaps, or disrupt the direction already established by the main strokes.

Main strokes establish structure; supporting strokes add detail and connections. Together, they help the brow retain both definition and softness.

09｜Finish with fine strokes

The final step is to enrich the layers within the brow with more delicate, fine strokes.

These strokes are usually lighter, shorter and paler. Their main role is to suggest subtle variations in hair between the main and supporting strokes.

Create natural transitions in tone, density and the balance of defined and subtle lines. Keep the overall contour in mind and avoid letting fine strokes stray beyond the lower edge.

Fine strokes are not there to make the brow denser. They add lightness and detail between the structural lines.

When main, supporting and fine strokes connect naturally, the brow can present a clear framework, structure and layers while retaining the visual character of natural hair flow.

Brow design ultimately needs to consider the whole face

The nine illustrations above show one approach to arranging hair-stroke brow lines on paper.

An actual brow design, however, cannot be decided from a brow diagram alone.

Lele considers the depth, visual weight and overall character of the facial features, the height of the cheekbones and brow bone, and the direction of the existing brow hairs.

Working with the existing bone structure, she uses the brow contour to refine facial proportions visually. With a suitable technique, main strokes establish structure and fine strokes enrich the layers, bringing the brows into greater harmony with the other features.

Lele favours soft, natural brows that retain the character of the original hair, but clients are not limited to one style.

Assess the bone structure, then define the contour; establish the structure, then develop the layers.

This is why the framework and the strokes need to work together in hair-stroke brow design.

Note: These images illustrate brow design and stroke arrangement on paper. They are not client procedure examples or instructions for performing cosmetic pigmentation yourself. Actual design and technique choices must be assessed in relation to the individual’s starting point.
