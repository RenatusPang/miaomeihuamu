Working with old brow pigment

When an older brow tattoo has left red, blue or grey residue, adding new hair strokes on top does not automatically mask it. Assessing undertone, width and shape boundaries comes first.

Why older brow tattoos fade to red, blue or grey undertones

Residual red, blue or grey tones relate to older pigment formulations and placement depth. Because residual ink sits inside the skin, ultra-fine hair strokes placed softly on top cannot erase a solid dark block underneath.

Four points checked before redesigning over old pigment

An in-person check examines four factors: undertone and saturation (reddish vs bluish-grey), whether the old outline is too wide or drooping, how much natural hair remains to blend strokes, and whether a refined new contour can sensibly incorporate the existing footprint.

Studio boundaries: not our primary service and no removal offered

To be transparent, bespoke virgin hair-stroke brows are our main focus; corrective cover-ups are assessed case by case and accepted only when visually viable. We do not perform laser or chemical pigment removal—if heavy or misplaced old ink prevents clean redesign, we advise consulting a qualified medical provider first.

If faint old colour qualifies for direct adjustment

When old pigment is light and sits within a workable bone-structure zone, gentle tonal balancing plus natural hair-flow lines may be discussed during a free 30–45 minute trial drawing, always subject to honest suitability review and backed by our one-year free touch-up.
